# Tochhawng Virtual Laboratory inventory

The independent laboratory route is `/laboratory`. Existing JEE and NEET class demonstrations remain available in their original lessons.

| Area | Interactive modules shipped on the laboratory route | Remaining scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physics | Ideal projectile motion, small-angle pendulum, a two-resistor DC circuit, a force-and-friction bench and ideal-interface reflection and refraction. Variable controls, live measurements, reset and practice. Motion also supports playback and time selection. | Extended Newtonian dynamics, gravity, circular motion, waves, circuit extensions, magnetism and lenses. Reuse the existing course experiments where appropriate. |
| Chemistry | Particle-count model for isotopes and ions, including changes to mass number and charge. | Mathematics | Quadratic graphs with adjustable vertex, curvature and direction, and computed real roots. | Other functions, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, probability, statistics, vectors and coordinate geometry. |
| Political science and history | Illustrative 100-seat assembly with adjustable yes and no votes and a live simple-majority threshold. Constitution interaction remains in the study room. | Constitutional institutions, political theory, history and Mizoram timelines. |

The current motion models assume constant gravity and equal launch and landing heights for projectiles, and small angles and negligible friction for the pendulum. The duration, range, and period are tested numerically. Student controls have keyboard alternatives and reduced motion uses manual time control. Connect new modules to reviewed course notes and questions and retain a text equivalent for each visualization.

## Educational quality review

Reviewed the 12 models against the assumptions in their controls and explanations. Projectile and pendulum preserve the equal-height and small-angle assumptions. The circuit uses ideal components. Friction is a from-rest comparison with a fixed kinetic/static ratio. Refraction uses an ideal planar interface and indices that are illustrative. The atom counter displays mass number and charge without inferring isotope stability. The pea table predicts probabilities for a one-gene cross. The quadratic model uses nonzero curvature. The assembly illustrates a simple majority of votes cast and leaves quorum and special voting procedures outside the model. The meridian model uses a spherical Earth and same-longitude separation.

The cell model now draws the photosynthetic plant plasma membrane close to the wall, adds the central vacuole and identifies chloroplasts as specific to photosynthetic tissues. The molecular bench distinguishes its displayed atom positions from lone pairs and atomic sizes. The refraction bench links to the dedicated optics companion. These refinements retain existing controls and quiz interactions.
